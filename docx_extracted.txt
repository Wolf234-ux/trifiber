snacking

Yet most fiber products remain positioned primarily around constipation.

cravings

SLIDE 14 — WHAT WE WILL NOT SAY
We Will Not Sell “Natural Ozempic.”
Ozempic is a prescription medication containing semaglutide.
Berberine is not semaglutide.
Its mechanism, pharmacology, evidence and expected magnitude of effect are different.
Recent research suggests possible metabolic and modest body-composition effects from berberine, while government health guidance continues to emphasize limitations in the evidence.
Therefore:
“Natural Ozempic” is the cultural insight.It is not the product claim.
Our positioning:
Botanical Metabolic Support
or
Daily Metabolic Balance
This allows us to participate in the same consumer conversation without misleading consumers or creating unnecessary regulatory exposure.

SLIDE 17 — WHAT THE SCIENCE ACTUALLY SAYS
Berberine Is Interesting—But We Should Not Oversell It
Recent systematic evidence suggests berberine may influence several metabolic parameters.
A 2025 meta-analysis of randomized trials reported statistically significant but relatively modest average reductions in body weight and BMI.
Government health guidance also notes evidence suggesting possible effects on glucose and cholesterol-related parameters.
However:
studies use different doses
formulations vary
study populations vary
many studies are relatively small
weight-loss effects are far smaller than those associated with modern prescription GLP-1 therapies
more high-quality research is needed
That is exactly why our R&D matters.
Instead of using the scientific uncertainty as a marketing loophole:
We use it as a product-development opportunity.
SLIDE 18 — BERBERINE SAFETY IS PART OF THE PRODUCT
Responsible Supplement Design Is a Competitive Advantage
Berberine can cause gastrointestinal side effects including:
nausea
abdominal discomfort
bloating
diarrhea
constipation
It may also interact with medications.
It should not be positioned for use during pregnancy or breastfeeding, and additional safety exclusions will follow regulatory and medical review.
Our product strategy therefore includes:
clear warnings
gradual-use guidance if appropriate
medication interaction warning
adverse-event reporting process
QR-linked education
physician/scientific advisory review
batch-specific quality testing
Trust is part of the formulation.

Oat Beta-Glucan
U.S. regulation 21 CFR §101.81 recognizes specific conditions for soluble-fiber health claims involving eligible oat/barley beta-glucan sources and psyllium.
The regulation identifies approximately:
3 g/day beta-glucan soluble fiber
and
7 g/day soluble fiber from psyllium seed husk
in the context of the authorized coronary-heart-disease claim and all associated qualifying conditions.
This does not mean our initial TriFiber formula automatically qualifies.
The commercial formula must be analyzed and reviewed before using such claims.
United States Supplement Claims
FDA dietary supplements may use appropriately substantiated structure/function claims but cannot claim to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease without becoming subject to drug requirements.
FDA specifically notes that referencing a particular therapy or drug action may create a disease claim.
This is a key reason we should not market Berberine as:
“Natural Ozempic.”
U.S. Advertising
FTC requires health-related advertising to be truthful, not misleading and adequately substantiated.
Implied claims count—not only literal wording.
India Fiber Claims
Current FSSAI criteria specify thresholds for claims such as:
Source of dietary fibre
and
High/Rich in dietary fibre.
Final claims depend on laboratory-verified finished-product composition.
European Union
Permitted nutrition claims are defined under Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006.
For example, EU criteria for “high fibre” include at least:
6 g fiber per 100 g
or
3 g fiber per 100 kcal.
Health claims must follow the applicable authorized-claims framework.
European Berberine Position
EFSA has been conducting a safety evaluation of plant preparations containing berberine and related protoberberine alkaloids.
Therefore we should treat Europe as a later-stage, market-specific launch for the berberine SKU rather than assuming a single global formulation.
UAE
Health supplements require applicable registration/marketing authorization.
Healthcare products promoted for general health are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease.
Disease-style claims should therefore not be part of the product proposition.
